What this app does

GoPro NAS Sync copies the photos and videos from your GoPro to your NAS, without a phone, a computer or the Quik app. You press Sync now (or let an automation do it), and the Raspberry Pi wakes the camera, copies every file it hasn't copied before, and puts the camera back to sleep.

  • Only new files are copied. The app remembers what it already copied, even after you format the SD card.
  • The files stay on the camera. Delete them on the camera yourself once you're happy.
  • Files go straight to the NAS. Nothing is stored on the Pi, so its SD card never fills up.

How it works

The Pi uses three different connections. The camera never touches your home network, and the NAS never talks to the camera.

  1. 1
    You start a sync on this web page, from your phone, or from an automation such as Home Assistant. The request reaches the Pi over your home network.
  2. 2
    Bluetooth: the Pi checks that the NAS is ready, then wakes the camera over Bluetooth and asks it to switch on its own Wi-Fi. Bluetooth also keeps the camera awake during the copy and puts it back to sleep at the end.
  3. 3
    Camera Wi-Fi: the Pi joins the camera's Wi-Fi network, reads the list of files and downloads the new ones. This is why the Pi's Wi-Fi is reserved for the camera and must not be connected to your home Wi-Fi.
  4. 4
    Ethernet: while downloading, the Pi writes each file directly to the NAS share through its network cable.

What you need

Keep the Pi within a few metres of where the camera usually lies (for example its charging spot). Walls slow the camera Wi-Fi down.

Setup, step by step

Do the steps in this order. The NAS comes first because the app refuses to wake the camera when it has nowhere to put the files.

  1. 0InstallDone, you're reading this on the Pi.
  2. 1Connect the NASNAS tab: enter the share, test, save.
  3. 2Prepare the cameraTurn on wireless, choose the Wi-Fi band.
  4. 3Pair the cameraCameras tab: once per camera.
  5. 4Copy your filesStatus tab: Sync now.

Step 1: Connect the NAS

On the NAS, prepare a shared folder for your GoPro files and a user that may write to it. On a Synology NAS (DSM):

  1. Control Panel > Shared Folder > Create, for example a folder named video.
  2. Control Panel > User & Group: create a user (for example gopro) and give it Read/Write permission on that folder.
  3. SMB is switched on by default (Control Panel > File Services > SMB).

In this app, open the NAS tab and fill in:

TypeSMB / CIFS share for Synology, QNAP, Windows and most NAS. Choose NFS only if you know you use it.
ServerThe NAS address, for example 192.168.1.10 or diskstation.local. Find it in your router or in Synology Assistant. The NAS must be on your wired network.
Share nameThe shared folder's name, for example video. For NFS, the export path instead, for example /volume1/video.
Protocol versionLeave empty. Only set it (3.0, 2.1) if the test complains.
Username, passwordThe NAS user from above. The password is stored on the Pi, readable only by the system.
Sub directoryA folder inside the share for the GoPro files, GoPro by default. It's created if needed.
Folder layoutHow files are sorted. The default {camera}/{date} gives GoPro/HERO11 Black 2106/2026-10-18/GX010034.MP4. Also available: {year}, {month}, {folder}.

Click Test connection: it checks that the NAS answers and that the Pi reaches it through the cable. Then click Save and mount. The Status tab should now show Mounted and writable. The share is mounted again automatically after a reboot.

Already mounted something yourself? Choose Existing directory and enter its path.

Step 2: Prepare the camera

Do this once on the camera. Menu names below are for the HERO10, HERO11, HERO12 and HERO13: swipe down from the top of the back screen, then tap Preferences. On HERO9 and HERO11 Mini the same items are under swipe down > Connections. Names may differ slightly between firmware versions.

Wireless onPreferences > Wireless Connections > Wireless Connections: On. Without it the camera can't be woken over Bluetooth.
Wi-Fi bandPreferences > Wireless Connections > Wi-Fi Band. Choose 2.4 GHz if your Pi is a Pi 3 Model B or a Zero 2 W: they can't see 5 GHz networks, and the sync then fails with "Wi-Fi network not found". A Pi 3B+, 4 or 5 can use 5 GHz, which is faster when the camera is close.
Camera namePreferences > Wireless Connections > Camera Info shows the camera's name and Wi-Fi password. The app reads them itself, so you don't have to note them.
FirmwareKeep the camera up to date with the Quik app. HERO9 needs at least 1.60.

Step 3: Pair the camera

  1. Switch the camera on and keep it close to the Pi.
  2. On the camera: Preferences > Wireless Connections > Connect Device > GoPro Quik App (HERO9 and HERO11 Mini: swipe down > Connections > Connect Device > GoPro Quik App). The camera now shows that it is waiting for a device.
  3. In the Cameras tab, click Pair camera within about a minute.
  4. The camera reports paired, and it appears in the list with its model, serial number and firmware. You can rename it there. The name is used for its folder on the NAS.

Pairing is stored on the Pi. You only do it again after resetting the camera's connections or clicking Forget. You can pair several cameras; a sync copies them one after the other. Untick Enabled to skip a camera.

Step 4: Copy your files

Put the camera in reach of the Pi and switch it off normally (it then goes into standby), then click Sync now in the Status tab. You'll see each step: NAS check, waking the camera, joining its Wi-Fi, reading the file list, then the copy with progress, speed and time remaining. At the end the camera goes back to sleep.

How long does it take? On a Pi 3 the camera Wi-Fi delivers about 5 MB/s, roughly 20 GB per hour: a 4 GB video takes about 12 minutes. The first sync of a full SD card can take several hours. Later syncs only copy what's new.

Stopping: Stop keeps everything already copied; the file in progress is dropped and copied again next time. Interrupted copies are also resumed automatically after short Wi-Fi drops.

Start a sync without opening this page

Any device on your network can start a sync with an HTTP POST request:

curl -X POST http://<pi-address>:8080/api/sync
  • Home Assistant, for example when the camera is put on its charger: 
    rest_command:
  gopro_sync:
    url: "http://<pi-address>:8080/api/sync"
    method: post
  • iPhone / Android: a Shortcut (or HTTP Shortcuts app) with Get contents of URL, method POST, on the address above.
  • Every night: a cron job on any computer, or on the Pi itself: gopro-sync sync.

What to look out for

  • Wi-Fi band: 2.4 GHz on the camera if the Pi is a Pi 3 Model B or Zero 2 W.
  • Cable for the Pi: the Pi must be connected by Ethernet. Its Wi-Fi belongs to the camera; don't connect it to your home Wi-Fi.
  • Standby, not off for days: the camera can only be woken over Bluetooth for some hours after it was switched off. After a long pause, switch it on once by hand before syncing.
  • Battery: a long copy drains the battery. Plug the camera into USB power for the first, big sync.
  • One connection at a time: close the Quik app on your phone (or switch off the phone's Bluetooth) while syncing. The camera talks to one device at a time.
  • Distance: a few metres, ideally in the same room. Speed drops quickly through walls.
  • Don't record during a sync. Press Stop first if you need the camera.
  • Free space: the app checks that the NAS has room for all new files before copying anything.

Files already copied

The History tab lists every file copied so far: where it came from, its size and where it is on the NAS. A file in this list is never copied again, even if you rename or move it on the NAS.

  • Re-transfer next to a file removes it from the list, so the next sync copies it again (for example after you deleted it on the NAS by mistake).
  • Reset list removes all entries (or those of the selected camera): everything still on the camera is copied again.

The app recognises a file by camera, name and recording time. GoPro reuses names like GX010001.MP4 after formatting the SD card; those new files are still copied.

Troubleshooting

The Status tab shows which step failed, and Last run > Steps keeps the details. Most problems are solved by switching the camera on by hand and clicking Sync now again.

The NAS step fails ("not mounted", "not writable", "route")
Check the Pi's network cable and that the NAS is on. In the NAS tab, click Test connection; for "permission denied" check the user's Read/Write permission on the shared folder.
"Wake camera over Bluetooth" fails or keeps retrying
The camera is out of range, its wireless is off, or it has been off too long to be woken. Switch it on by hand, close the Quik app on your phone, and try again.
"The camera's Wi-Fi network ... was not found"
Almost always the Wi-Fi band: set the camera to 2.4 GHz (see Step 2). Otherwise the camera is too far away.
"No answer from the camera within ... s"
The camera stopped responding. Switch it off and on by hand and sync again; files already copied are kept.
The copy is interrupted ("Connection to camera lost")
Short drops are retried automatically. If it keeps happening, move the camera closer to the Pi and plug it into power, then sync again: the copy continues with the remaining files.
Pairing fails
Make sure the camera shows the pairing screen when you click Pair camera, and that no phone is connected to it. If it still fails, reset the camera's connections (Preferences > Wireless Connections > Reset Connections), then pair again. You'll need to pair the Quik app again too.
A camera shows up twice or with the wrong name
Rename it in the Cameras tab, or Forget the extra entry. Its history is kept.
Something else
The detailed log is on the Pi: journalctl -u gopro-sync -f.
